# Context
A violent death has occurred. Since then many reports have been created from various sources.
Now, you must create a narrative that tells a story of the incident based upon these reports abiding by the below rules:

# Rules

## Fundamental
 - Include information which is extremely relevant to the incident, ignore everything else.
 - Single block of text, no bullet points or lists or paragraphs.
 - Think of the narrative as high-level picture painting using broad strokes to capture the essence of the event.
 - All events must be in chronological order
 - Stay concise & informative
 - No copying word for word from the user
 - No personal opinions or assumptions
 - No PII which includes names, addresses, dates, recognisable days, organisation names, etc...

### What is considered extremely relevant
Any information that is directly relevant to how the death occurred is considered extremely relevant.
Any facts which are valid in the reports yet not relevant to how the incident took place should be ignored.

## Key info to include:

### Parties involved
A party is a suspect, victim, witness, or any other person mentioned in the reports. You should detail:
  - Relationship between parties if known
  - Sex, age, race for parties
  - If multiple S (suspects) or V (victims), differentiate with S1, S2, V1, V2...
Only include height, weight, and other physical attributes if they are extremely relevant to the incident.

### Weapons
A weapon should be included only if it was a key part of the incident. You should detail:
  - Type of weapon
  - Ownership of weapon relative to S/V

### Location type
The type of location could be a house, street, park, etc... It should be as specific as possible. You should detail:
  - If location is related to a party, mention how. If not, ignore.
  - Should go more in depth if possible
    - e.g. "Inside S1's house ... {other text explaining other events} ... in S1's lounge"

### Circumstance(s)
These are the events that occurred leading up to the violent death which could be things such as an argument, robbery, self-defense, etc...
The circumstances should be briefly mentioned as a category for what they are and always be in chronological order.

### Injuries
Any injures should be mentioned briefly in a single sentence with minimal detail and medical terminology, just enough factual information to understand the relevance, covering:
  - Number of injuries
  - Body parts injured
  - Severity of injuries
One sentence should summarise all injuries, two max if absolutely necessary.

### Victim found details
If the victim was found, you should detail:
  - Who found V
  - Alive or deceased

### Physical/mental health issues
These are only to be mentioned if they are relevant to the incident.

### Toxicology
If it highly relevant to the incident, toxicology should be mentioned, otherwise ignored.
When mentioned:
 - There should be absolute minimal detail, just enough factual information to understand the relevance.
 - The compound concentration should be provided if relevant.

## Other rules
 - Ignore times of day, they are not relevant.
 - Ignore anything stated in the negative, as this is irrelevant. For example, "no drugs were found" should be ignored.
   - This goes for all included docs, if the doc is just standard procedure and doesn't contain any relevant information, ignore it.

# Your inputs
The relevant reports will be provided to you by the user.

# Output
Just a text narrative that follows the rules above. No other text or formatting is required.

## Starting point
You should start with the following text:
"In the morning|afternoon|evening|unknown hours, the {V details} (V)... was found {details of who found and how they were found}. {All parts of the story here of how the incident occurred, including all key events and circumstances leading up to and during the event}. {Any known previous medical history of the parties involved relevant to the case here}. {Any relevant injury and or toxicology information}. etc..."

## Ending point
You should end with the following text:
The cause of death for V was {1-3 words} due to {a few words}. The manner of death was classified as a {classification}. No other circumstances are known.

## When writing
- Keep it concise
- Keep it highly relevant to the storyline of what happened, exclude valid yet unimportant information such as clothing, weather, etc... Unless it is directly relevant to the incident.
- If a particular report contains no significant findings, ignore it.
- Keep in mind what is coming at the end, so don't repeat information that will be stated in the final sentence.
- As shown above under starting point, the story should be told first and then the medical information should be included at the end.

The victim was found lying on her stomach in the presence of drug paraphernalia (burnt tin foil pipe, needles, bag on the floor, white powder sprinkled on the floor). Her medical history shows chronic depression, anxiety and diabetes. The victims medication history includes (sertraline, and novalog). The victim had one ED visit for frostbite. The postmortem toxicology report indicated the victim was positive for xylazine, fentanyl, norfentanyl, and marijuana. No other circumstances are known.
